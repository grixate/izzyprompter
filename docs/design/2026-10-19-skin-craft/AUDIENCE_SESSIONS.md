# Skin craft — participant session guide

Prepared 19 October 2026. **No participant sessions have taken place.** This is the review kit, not results or evidence of purchase uplift.

Recruit 12–16 people who recently recorded prepared speech: solo video creators, experts/educators, audio authors and video operators. Include people who do not collect audio/camera hardware. Use participant codes P01–P16; do not put contact details or their real script material into this repository.

## Same conditions for every comparison

Use the same phone, synthetic script, initial position, 0.86× speed, framing and lighting. Keep the existing PRO offer and other benefits identical. Explain that the remote controls script playback and is not a recorder or sound player. Do not introduce the designer's favourite or the mechanism's story before the first choice. Rotate presentation order. A participant can choose none.

For a fair detail comparison show: the archived Groove, the refined Groove paused, then the same refined Groove running; alternate the order of the first two across sessions. The archived version is not a different commercial offer. All six new primary directions should be shown at comparable fidelity.

## Questions and tasks (about 20–25 minutes)

1. What prepared speech did you last record, and what was awkward about controlling it?
2. Looking at the instruments, which would you choose for yourself? You may choose up to three, or none. Why? Which feel too similar? Do not explain names yet.
3. On the chosen remote: start, pause, return to the preceding thought, change speed and resume. Observe accidental Play/jog actions and hesitation. Do not coach unless necessary; record the assistance.
4. After a short actual take: what did you notice? What would you keep or change? Did any movement distract you?
5. Compare the three Groove conditions above. What difference does each make to your preference? Avoid asking whether the tonearm is worth paying for before they have described its value themselves.
6. Compare Clear and Spool, then Glaze, Pop, Moss and Mono. Which, if any, gives you a choice missing from the original collection? Transparency is a product requirement; the question is which construction works best.
7. Use the equal-geometry rendered A/B palette pairs in the review gallery for the colour comparison; the vector sheets are only schematic orientation. Conceal material/name labels and rotate A/B positions for participants. B remains an unaccepted trial and needs the same contrast/functional checks if selected. Compare two palette treatments of the same construction under the same light. Do not change geometry and palette together.
8. Show the actual current PRO offer including workflow benefits. What would you choose, and why? Which benefits contribute to that decision? Separate purchase interest from liking a picture. Do not create a real order or charge during the session.
9. Which instrument would you use for your next take? If convenient, ask them to choose again after a later real recording, without a reminder of their first answer.

## Record and synthesize

Use `participant-results.csv`. Keep unsuccessful tasks and a “none” choice. Record direct observations separately from researcher interpretation. Do not fill missing entries by inference. Report counts as “4 of these 12 participants”, not a market share estimate. Look for repeated reasons, failed tasks, choices missing from the old set and whether the initial favourite survives use.

A favourable small study does not measure conversion. A purchase experiment needs an operating collection/offer, confirmed paid outcomes, a valid visit denominator, sufficient actual traffic and a predefined stopping rule. The current product event adapter is local-only; verify collection before proposing a numerical uplift.
